Choosing transport

  • No JR Pass. The 7-day pass is ¥50,000 ($318), and your two Shinkansen legs come to only ¥26,000 ($166)
  • Book Shinkansen reserved seats on SmartEX, with a child seat for him
  • Get the 2-day Freepass in Hakone; it covers trains, cable car, boat and buses
  • Use Suica in the cities. Under 6 rides the subway, regular JR trains and buses free
  • In Kyoto, take taxis to Kiyomizu-dera and Kinkaku-ji; easier with a child than a crowded bus
  • On arrival, Keikyu to Shinagawa, 15 minutes. On the way home, the airport limousine bus: bags go underneath, no dragging suitcases through the subway

Using Suica

  • Can be set up before leaving: iPhone Wallet → plus sign → Transit Card → Suica, load ¥5,000 ($32) with Apple Pay
  • Tap the phone on the gate going in and out, no unlocking needed. Works on the subway, JR, private railways, buses, convenience stores and vending machines
  • Alex is under 6 and needs no ticket; he just walks through the gate with you
  • Top up in Wallet anytime the balance runs low

Booking and riding the Shinkansen

  • Download SmartEX and register with a US credit card. Sales open 1 month before travel, so the 12/3 train can be booked from 11/3
  • Choose "Reserved seat", 2 seats: 1 adult, 1 child. Pick seats D and E together; E is by the window
  • After booking, the app shows a QR code; scan it at the Shinkansen gates
  • Shinagawa Station: after getting off the Keikyu or JR, follow the blue "Shinkansen" signs, 5 minutes inside the station
  • Carry-ons fit on the overhead rack
  • Eating on board is fine; buy a bento in the station before boarding

Getting around Kyoto

  • The hotel is at Kyoto Station (京都駅); Kintetsu (近鉄) to Nara and the JR Sagano Line (嵯峨野線) to Arashiyama both leave from Kyoto Station
  • Taxis: there's a taxi line in front of the station. Show the driver the Japanese place name on the phone. Cards accepted, and the doors are automatic, so no need to open or close them
  • Taxis are hard to hail for the trip back from Kiyomizu-dera and Kinkaku-ji; use GO or Uber
  • City buses are crowded and slow; avoid them with a child when possible

In Kyoto, buy small things with a Kyoto character. Save clothes, electronics, toys and anything big for Tokyo, and pack them into the new suitcase. Spend ¥5,000 ($32) or more in one store for a tax refund; bring the passport.

Where to buy LV and Hermès

  • Buy in Ginza on Sunday afternoon, 12/13: LV Namiki-dori and Hermès Ginza are both flagships with the fullest stock
  • Look on the way through Omotesando on 12/12: LV Omotesando and Hermès Omotesando, 5 minutes' walk from Kiddy Land
  • Shinjuku Isetan also has LV and Hermès, open until 8 pm, any evening
  • Kyoto Takashimaya has them too, passing by on the evening of 12/4. Save big purchases for Tokyo so you don't carry them to Hakone
  • Hermès Birkins and Kellys are basically never in stock; other bags, scarves and small leather goods depend on that day's stock

Clothes for you and him

  • 12/13 Minami-Machida Grandberry Park outlets: 200-plus stores, cheaper than department stores; the main place to buy
  • Shinjuku Isetan: kids' clothes on the 6th floor of the main building, with Japanese brands like Miki House. Women's on 2 to 4
  • NEWoMan and Lumine at Shinjuku Station South exit: lots of Japanese women's brands
  • Ginza: the 12-floor Uniqlo flagship, plus GU and Muji flagships, basics for adults and kids
  • Kyoto Station Isetan and Shijo Takashimaya, if you're passing by

Paying, tax refunds, US customs

  • Bring the passport. From November 1, 2026, Japan switched to paying the tax-inclusive price, with the 10% consumption tax refunded after a check at the airport on departure
  • On 12/15 at Haneda, do the tax refund check first, then check the bag. The purchases have to leave the country with you
  • Use a credit card with no foreign transaction fee, and choose to pay in yen
  • Each person has an $800 duty-free allowance returning to the US, and you and he can combine to $1,600. Duty is owed on anything over; declare honestly on entry and keep the receipts
  • Before leaving, check yen prices on the LV and Hermès Japan sites, compare with US prices, then decide what to buy

Gachapon and cute shops

  • 12/12 Gashapon Department Store in Ikebukuro Sunshine City: 3,000 machines, the most in the world
  • 12/12 #C-pla on Takeshita Street in Harajuku: two floors, about 850 kinds
  • 12/9 Shimokitazawa: little gift shops and vintage clothing, one alley after another
  • 12/4 Shinkyogoku and Teramachi shopping arcades in Kyoto: gachapon shops and little gift shops
  • 12/3 Donguri Republic on Ninenzaka in Kyoto, 12/6 Snoopy Chaya in Nishiki Market
  • 12/5 Miffy Sakura Kitchen and Rilakkuma Saryo in Arashiyama
  • ¥300 ($1.9) to ¥500 ($3.2) a turn, 100-yen coins only; shops have change machines
