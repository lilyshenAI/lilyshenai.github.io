Research

I study how information moves real estate and mortgage markets. Much of my recent work uses machine learning and large language models as measurement tools: turning listing remarks, securitization contracts, and other unstructured text into auditable economic measures, then asking what those measures reveal about prices, liquidity, risk, and the intermediaries who connect buyers, sellers, and lenders. Other work covers mortgage default, tenant rights and rent affordability, flood-insurance repricing, political alignment in neighborhood choice, real estate agents, and manufacturing reshoring.

Tenant Rights, Eviction, and Rent Affordability

N. Edward Coulson, Thao Le, Victor Ortego-Marti, Lily Shen · Journal of Urban Economics, 147, 103762 · 2025 · [ABDC A*]

We use state-level differences in the legal relationship between landlords and tenants to estimate the impact of these differences on housing markets. We construct a search-theoretic model of landlord and tenant search and matching, which predicts that an increase in the cost of eviction reduces the number of evictions, but raises rents and homeless rates, and lowers housing supply and vacancy rates. To test these predictions, we construct an annual index to measure the level of the legal protection of tenant rights in each state. Our instrumental variable results indicate that while a one-unit increase in the Tenant-Right Index reduces the eviction rate by 0.32 percentage points, rental housing is 1.8 percent more expensive in areas where tenants have more protections against landlords. A higher Tenant-Right Index is also associated with a decrease in housing supply and an increase in the homeless rate. Taken together, our findings highlight a significant trade-off between tenant protections and rent affordability.

The More, the Merrier: Agent Connectedness in Real Estate Trading Networks

Lily Shen, Xiaojin Sun · Journal of Real Estate Research, 47(2), 149-169 · 2025 · [ABDC B] · American Real Estate Society Best Article Award

This study applies network theory to examine how real estate agents' connections influence transaction outcomes, using data from over 40,000 sales in Atlanta, GA. It finds that agents with stronger networks achieve higher sales prices, with a one standard deviation increase in connectedness leading to a 2.61% price boost. These well-connected agents sell unique properties faster and more accurately price them, suggesting that their networks enhance market efficiency through improved information flow.

Information in Financial Contracts: Evidence from Securitization Agreements

Brent W. Ambrose, Yiqiang Han, Sanket Korgaonkar, Lily Shen · Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, 59(4), 1692-1725 · 2024 · [ABDC A*]

We introduce a novel application of machine learning to compare Pooling and Servicing Agreements (PSAs) that govern commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS). In contrast to the view that the PSA is largely boilerplate text, we document substantial variation across PSAs, both within- and across-underwriters and over time. A part of this variation is driven by differences in loan collateral across deals. Additionally, we find that differences in PSAs are correlated with ex-post loan and bond performance. Collectively, our analysis suggests the importance of examining the entire governing document, rather than specific components, when analyzing complex financial securities.

Past Experiences and Investment Decisions: Evidence from Real Estate Markets

Brent W. Ambrose, Lily Shen · Journal of Real Estate Finance and Economics, 66(2), 300-326 · 2023 · [ABDC A]

This paper investigates how market participants form their risk perspectives about the real estate market through a sequence of information shocks. Guided by a theoretical Bayesian learning model, we exploit a natural experiment afforded by the fracking boom in Pennsylvania in the late 2000s. We empirically examine whether familiarity with conventional gas exploration affects home-buyers' willingness to pay for houses near fracking wells.

Cleanliness is Next to Income: The Impact of COVID-19 on Short-Term Rentals

Lily Shen, Sean Wilkoff · Journal of Regional Science, 62(3), 799-829 · 2022 · [ABDC A] · Featured by The Economist, December 2020

We use Airbnb data from Austin, Texas to empirically investigate the impact of the onset of COVID-19 on the short-term rental market. Specifically, we employ a machine learning algorithm to create an extensive cleanliness dictionary to detect whether an Airbnb unit is clean. We use a difference-in-difference specification to value the change in income related to reviewer-perceived cleanliness during the COVID-19 pandemic. We find the following results: First, available listings declined by 25% once the pandemic hit and those that remained lost 22% of their income and had occupancy decrease by 20%. Second, properties that were perceived to be clean increased their income by 17.5% and their occupancy by 16.5%, mitigating the negative shock due to COVID-19. Third, rental prices for clean Airbnb listings did not increase after COVID-19. In addition, we study the interaction of Airbnb supply on the long-term rental market during a market decline.

The Odd One Out? The Impact of Property Uniqueness on Selling Time and Selling Price

Lily Shen, Thomas M. Springer · Journal of Housing Research, 31(2), 220-240 · 2022 · [ABDC B] · Best Paper Award, Journal of Housing Research, 2022 volume

We employ machine learning to develop measures of residential real estate uniqueness from written advertisements. These measures are exogenous from sales prices. We distinguish the effect of market uniqueness (compared to houses for sale at the same time) from the effect of universal uniqueness (compared to houses in the same sub-market) on sale prices and time-on-the-market (TOM). The hedonic models show that a one standard deviation increase in market uniqueness leads to a 13% ($48,490) increase in sale prices at the cost of delaying the transaction for 1.7 days, whereas a one standard deviation increase in universal uniqueness only leads to an 11% ($41,030) increase in sale prices at the cost of delaying the transaction for 3 days. We validated the impact of uniqueness on TOM using two hazard models. Our results highlight the importance of uniqueness and market timing in real estate.

Information Value of Property Description: A Machine Learning Approach

Lily Shen, Stephen L. Ross · Journal of Urban Economics, 121, 103299 · 2021 · [ABDC A*]

This paper employs machine learning to quantify the value of "soft" information contained in real estate property descriptions. Textual descriptions contain information that traditional hedonic attributes cannot capture. A one standard deviation increase in the uniqueness of a property based on this "soft" information leads to a 15% increase in property sale price in a hedonic price model and a 10% increase in a repeat sales price model. An annual hedonic price index ignoring our measure of unobserved quality overstates real estate prices by between 10% to 23% and mistimes the stabilization of housing prices following the Great Recession. Similar, but smaller effects, are observed for the repeat sales price index.

The Double Trigger for Mortgage Default: Evidence from the Fracking Boom

Chris Cunningham, Kristopher Gerardi, Lily Shen · Management Science, 67(6), 3943-3964 · 2021 · [ABDC A*]

Our results show that homeowners exposed to fracking were significantly less likely to default on their loans. Instrumenting for fracking increases the magnitude of the effect appreciably, consistent with underlying endogeneity in the location of drilling activity. The fracking measures most associated with the labor-intensive act of creating wells have the largest net effects. Furthermore, we show that fracking activity significantly increased employment growth at the county level. These results are consistent with the double-trigger theory of mortgage default, which contends that liquidity shocks in addition to negative equity drive borrowers to default due to their inability to afford payments or sell their homes.

Economies of Scale and the Operating Efficiency of REITs: A Revisit

Michael J. Highfield, Lily Shen, Thomas M. Springer · Journal of Real Estate Finance and Economics, 62(1), 108-138 · 2021 · [ABDC A]

Building on past research in the economies of scale debate, and recognizing the substantial changes in this industry since the turn of the millennium, we use a time-varying stochastic frontier approach to monitor changes in REIT efficiency over the 2001-2015 period. While the stochastic frontier model suggests that the overall level of operating efficiency for US equity REITs is stable, using a series of linear models we document evidence that economies of scale still exist in this industry.

The Good, the Bad and the Ordinary: Estimating Agent Value-Added using Real Estate Transactions

Chris Cunningham, Kristopher Gerardi, Lily Shen · 2024 · Revise and resubmit, Management Science

We recover measures of real estate agents' value added in the housing transaction process using a detailed Multiple Listing Service (MLS) property transactions database covering three large metro areas. Controlling for property and location characteristics, we find considerable heterogeneity in the sale prices obtained by realtors as well as the time that it takes to complete a sale. Focusing on a sample of repeat sales, we estimate an inter-quartile price range for both listing and buying agents to be approximately 4 to 5 percent. The inter-quartile range for days-on-the-market is between 15 and 25 days. Listing agents that tend to sell for a premium do not take any longer to sell, indicating that agents do not have fixed price posting/acceptance strategies. Agents that sell homes at a premium also tend to pay more when acting as a buyer's agent, which is inconsistent with realtors being good negotiators. Finally, homes sold via a "flat-fee" agency on the MLS obtain prices that are 1 to 5 percent higher compared to those obtained by a traditional listing agent and take no longer to sell.

Cheap Talk or Priced Information? Listing Language and Housing Transaction Outcomes

Lily Shen · 2026 · Working paper, SSRN, June 2026

Using 63,067 Atlanta MLS transactions from 2007 to 2017, we examine what information listing remarks reveal and where it appears in housing transaction outcomes. An LLM scans the corpus and organizes broad listing language into auditable property and transaction categories. Within search-area-year markets and conditional on a saturated hedonic specification, a one-standard-deviation increase in residual information is associated with 7.7 percent higher sale prices, 7.6 percent higher list prices, and 2.5 fewer days on market. Adverse disclosure is associated with 17.4 percent lower sale prices, 16.7 percent lower list prices, and a 0.7 percent lower sale-to-list ratio. Urgency pressure is associated with 4.7 percent lower sale prices and 4.4 percent lower list prices, but not with a distinct sale-to-list adjustment or shorter marketing time. Market-stress language rises during the housing crisis and is more prevalent when local prices are lower, marketing time is longer, and discounts are larger. Repeat-sale and listing-agent specifications imply more conservative but economically meaningful price differences, and residual information and adverse disclosure retain pricing content after LASSO-selected token controls. The findings show that remarks reveal priced property and transaction information beyond structured MLS fields while identifying the economic categories in which that information appears.

Listing Language and House Price Discovery

Lily Shen · 2026 · Working paper

House-price indices summarize closed transactions, after marketing, bargaining, and failed listings have already been resolved. This paper asks whether public MLS remarks reveal local housing conditions earlier. From a 2.03 million listing corpus, I construct AI-assisted measures of three economically interpretable signals: demand heat, seller pressure, and asset frictions. The primary sample contains 0.81 million North and South Carolina listings on an aligned 2001-2019 coverage window; 0.83 million Georgia listings serve as an out-of-sample market. Remarks precede closing by a median of 84 days. In listing-level regressions with county-by-year and listing-agent fixed effects, demand-heat language predicts 1.5 percent higher sale prices, 9.7 fewer days on market, a 1.28 percentage point smaller discount from list price, and a 4.48 percentage point higher sale probability. Seller pressure and asset-friction language predict lower prices, longer marketing time, larger discounts, and lower sale probabilities. Aggregated to county-months, demand heat predicts 0.58 percentage points higher six-month MLS hedonic-index growth within state-month, reduces holdout forecast errors by 23 percent in the 2015-2019 expansion, and passes a geographic reassignment test. The same design replicates in Georgia: demand heat predicts 0.43 percentage points higher six-month price growth in a market never used for estimation. The evidence shows that listing language is not generic sentiment: it is typed market information observed months before a transaction-based HPI can incorporate the corresponding sales.

Soft Information and Price Discovery in Illiquid Asset Markets: Evidence from Housing

Lily Shen · 2026 · Working paper

How does price discovery work when assets are heterogeneous, trade infrequently, and benchmark prices are estimated from transactions that close with delay? Residential real estate is a useful setting because intermediaries describe assets when they are brought to market, while transaction-based indexes are formed from sales that close later. Using 63,067 transaction-level listings from 2007 to 2017, I measure local market conditions from listing-stage language and aggregate it to local-market-year cells. The measures validate as market-state signals: seller-stress language rises in weak periods, is higher in local markets with lower prices and worse liquidity, and varies across areas within the same aggregate cycle. The central result is a decomposition of what listing language reveals before transaction benchmarks update. Part of the signal reflects observable composition: controlling for the structured distressed-sale share (REO and corporate-owned listings) absorbs most of the seller-stress coefficient in next-year price-index regressions. But listing language is not exhausted by composition: the text channels are jointly significant beyond the distressed-sale share, current conditions, and year effects (F = 7.09, p = 0.003), with disclosed property-condition risk predicting weaker and buyer-competition language predicting stronger next-year local price-index changes. Listing remarks thus aggregate hard distress composition and residual soft information into a single public, real-time signal that appears before delayed transaction benchmarks record local deterioration.

Who Prices Flood Risk and When: Evidence from Housing Markets

Sirui Cai, Lily Shen · 2025 · Working paper; invited submission to the Journal of Real Estate Research, under review

We examine how housing and mortgage markets adjust to the National Flood Insurance Program's Risk Rating 2.0 reform in Florida, which replaced community-based flood insurance rates with property-specific premiums based on advanced risk models. We find that properties in tracts facing larger premium increases experienced an average 2 percent decline in sale prices and a 1 percent drop in loan approval rates, concentrated in high-risk and lower-income areas. The effects are stronger where households rely more on federally backed or non-bank lending, and non-bank lenders respond by raising rates and securitizing a greater share of loans, suggesting that financial intermediaries transmit newly revealed climate risk into property values and credit supply.

Political Alignment and Neighborhood Choice Among Homebuyers

Sirui Cai, Yongqiang Chu, Thao Le, Lily Shen · 2026 · Working paper

We study the impact of buyers' political identity on home purchasing using North Carolina voter records linked to more than 250,000 housing transactions and over six million comparable MLS listings. Using a Bartik-type shift-share IV framework, we find that buyers are significantly more likely to purchase homes in politically aligned neighborhoods, with alignment increasing purchase probability by about 1.58 percentage points relative to a 3 percent baseline. The effect is strongest among Republican buyers and declines sharply from 2010 to 2019 despite rising national polarization.

Impact of High-Skill Jobs on Commercial Real Estate

Sumit Agarwal, Brent W. Ambrose, Lily Shen · 2022 · Working paper

Job vacancies and labor demand are among the most critical drivers behind the value of local commercial properties. Using 4.8 million job advertisements from more than 40,000 career websites from 2010 to 2020, we measure labor demand trends within metro areas and assess their impact on CRE rent levels. We build time-varying measures for the demand of workers of high and low machine replacement risk and achieve identification by relying on the cross-sectional differences of tenants and property types to changes in labor skill demand. We document a positive relationship between employment growth and lease rates. For industrial properties, tenants who seek talent one year before the lease was signed pay 9.1% more than similar tenants who did not advertise any job vacancy. Office tenants who seek talent one year before pay 3.2% higher effective rents than tenants who did not announce any position. Those differences in rent levels are mainly driven by the demand for high-skill laborers that machines cannot replace.
