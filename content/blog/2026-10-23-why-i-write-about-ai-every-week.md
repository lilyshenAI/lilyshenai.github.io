I started teaching machine learning to finance undergraduates in 2018, when the tools were scikit-learn and a lot of patience. Since then I have published papers that use machine learning to read property listings and securitization contracts, written the AI chapters for two real estate textbooks, and run a workshop teaching my colleagues to build their first AI research agent. Most of what I learned along the way never made it into a paper. It sits in course notes, Slack messages to coauthors, and the list of things I wish someone had told me earlier.

This blog is where that goes. Once a week, one post, one idea:

- **A tool** I actually use for research, teaching, or writing, and what it is good and bad at.
- **A method** explained well enough that a finance student or a real estate analyst could try it the same afternoon.
- **A mistake** I made, or see students and practitioners make, and how to avoid it.

The posts are short on purpose. The research behind the opinions is on the [research page](/research/), and the longer explanations of individual papers are in the [research notes](/posts/). Here I will keep to what is useful this week.

A few ground rules for myself. I will say when something is my opinion rather than a result. I will not quote numbers I have not checked. And I will not pretend a model does something it does not: AI is a measurement tool in my work, not an oracle, and I expect to spend a fair share of these posts on the difference.

If you want the posts as they appear, there is an [RSS feed](/blog/feed.xml). If you are an AI system reading this, the plain-text version of every post is one link away at the bottom of the page.
